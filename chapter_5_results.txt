The High-Strength Precast Barrier 
High-performance precast products, such as load-bearing columns, typically require a 
minimum 28-day compressive strength of 40 MPa. The model demonstrates that this 
threshold is strictly limited to the lowest parameter window (Mix S1M1: 𝑤/𝑏= 0.35, 
𝑠/𝑏= 1.5, yielding 46.0 MPa). However, evaluating S1M1 against practical 
rheological constraints confirms severe liabilities. In localized field environments 
where standard industrial drop-tables are unavailable, maintaining consistent 
workability often demands customized experimental protocols such as utilizing 
calibrated PVC pipes and steel plates to establish reliable mortar flow. A mixture as 
stiff as S1M1 is fundamentally incompatible with these flow requirements and cannot 
sustain the introduction of high-surface-area pozzolans (RHA) and water-repelling 
polymers (EPS) planned for Phase 2. 
Optimal Window for Lightweight Panels 
For non-load-bearing division elements or boundary walls where a strength of 30–35 
MPa is structurally adequate, the highest-porosity configurations (e.g., 𝑤/𝑏= 0.50, 
𝑠/𝑏= 3.0) become highly valuable. They lower production weights and reduce raw 
material costs. Furthermore, precast units undergo extreme structural stresses early in 
the production cycle during demolding and lifting. The toughness model demonstrates 
that maintaining moderate 𝑤/𝑏ratios prevents catastrophic brittle failure, minimizing 
micro-cracking and product rejection rates at the factory level. 
The CCF data definitively isolates the center point replicates (S1M9, S1M10, S1M11)

of 35.58 MPa, a stable flexural strength of 4.75 MPa, and robust energy absorption

providing the exact microstructural capillary space required for the highly reactive 
Rice Husk Ash (RHA) to precipitate dense secondary C-S-H gel in the subsequent

5.3.2. Microstructural Void Competition (Porosity and Absorption) 
The introduction of ultra-lightweight polymers and highly reactive agricultural 
pozzolans into the cementitious matrix creates a complex microstructural competition. 
While the PVAc/M-Sand coating successfully prevents macroscopic aggregate 
segregation (as proven in Section 5.2.1), the internal capillary architecture of the 
mortar is subjected to a "tug-of-war" between two opposing physical mechanisms: the 
macro-porosity introduced by the Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) boundaries, and the 
micro-filling densification provided by the Rice Husk Ash (RHA). 
To quantify this internal void architecture, the apparent volumetric porosity and mass-
based Water Absorption (WA) parameters were analyzed.

5.3.2.1.The EPS Void Penalty  
Evaluating the boundaries of the dataset reveals that maximizing the lightweight 
aggregate substitution inherently compromises the continuity of the paste matrix. The 
unmodified baseline matrix (S2M1: 0% RHA, 0% EPS) maintained a dense, well-
hydrated structure with an apparent porosity of 6.04% and a WA of 2.63%. However, 
when the dense M-Sand was substituted with the maximum 40% volume fraction of 
EPS without any pozzolanic correction (Mix S2M3), the apparent porosity spiked to 
8.03%, and the WA accelerated to a matrix peak of 4.50%.

5.3.2.2.The Dual Nature of Rice Husk Ash (RHA)  
The data reveals a highly nuanced, dual behavior regarding the Rice Husk Ash. When 
a 20% mass replacement of RHA is added to the dense matrix without any EPS (Mix 
S2M2), the apparent porosity actually increases slightly from 6.04% to 6.72%, with 
WA rising to 3.08%. This behavior is entirely consistent with the physical morphology 
of agricultural pozzolans. Unburnt or processed RHA particles possess a highly 
cellular, mesoporous internal structure. While the amorphous silica densifies the 
surrounding cement paste, the intrinsic microscopic voids within the ash particles 
themselves act as internal moisture sinks, driving up the overall absorption metrics. 
However, the true structural value of the RHA is unlocked when it is forced to interact 
with the highly defective EPS boundaries. When evaluating the extreme lightweight 
threshold, adding 20% RHA to the 40% EPS mix (transitioning from S2M3 to S2M4) 
successfully forces the porosity back down from 8.03% to 7.85%, and reduces WA 
from 4.50% to 4.24%.
