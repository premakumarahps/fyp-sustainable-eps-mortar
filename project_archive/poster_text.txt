Lightweight mortars for precast ferrocement construction face an inherent engineering trade-off: reducing density with Expanded Polystyrene
(EPS) beads severely compromises compressive strength and toughness, driven by poor interfacial bonding and buoyancy-induced
segregation. This research resolves that paradox through a ternary synergistic system surface-coated EPS for density reduction, ball-milled
Rice Husk Ash (RHA) for pozzolanic matrix densification, and Polypropylene (PP) micro-fibers for crack-bridging toughness systematically
optimized via Response Surface Methodology with a Face-Centered Central Composite Design (CCF). The resulting matrix achieves a
density of 1993 kg/m³ (13% lighter than the plain control) while retaining a compressive strength of 15.62 MPa, flexural strength of 3.59
MPa, and toughness of 18.24 mJ/mm³, with apparent porosity essentially unchanged from the dense baseline. These results confirm that a
properly engineered ternary matrix can decouple density reduction from strength loss, offering a high-performance, viable lightweight
solution for non-load-bearing and also load bearing precast partition panels.

Introduction
Result and Discussion
The Paradox
Conventional cement mortar runs 2200–2400 kg/m³ heavy 
enough to demand high-capacity lifting equipment, increase 
handling stress during demolding, and risk early cracking before 
precast panels reach full strength. EPS beads are the obvious fix 
for weight, but raw EPS is a smooth, hydrophobic void that 
wrecks the very strength and toughness the mortar needs to 
survive that handling.
Microstructural Failure
Uncoated EPS creates a weak, calcium-rich Interfacial Transition 
Zone (ITZ) around every bead essentially a network of pre-
formed failure planes. Its low density also causes it to float and 
segregate during casting, compounding the problem before the 
mortar even sets.
The Objective
Engineer a ternary system that neutralizes all three failure modes 
at once: heal the ITZ chemically, lock the EPS mechanically into 
the paste, and arrest the resulting brittleness  without exceeding a 
target density of 2000 kg/m³.

A. Interfacial Transition Zone (ITZ) Densification 
SEM-EDS diagnostic mapping mathematically confirms the 
successful pozzolanic reaction of RHA: the elemental Calcium-
to-Silicon (Ca/Si) atomic ratio at the ITZ dropped from 24.85 to 
5.65  a ~77% reduction confirming the transformation of a weak, 
crystalline Portlandite-rich boundary into a dense, amorphous, 
load-bearing C-S-H gel.

B. Physical Coating & Compressive Recovery 
Surface coating eliminated the classic EPS "wall effect." At 40% 
EPS replacement, coating recovered compressive strength from 
4.20 to 7.15 MPa a 70.24% recovery proof that mechanical 
interlocking, not just chemistry, is essential to making EPS 
mortar viable.
Figure 6 : SEM Micrographs and strength comparison
C. Fiber Optimization & The Degradation Plateau 
On the optimized 10% RHA + 20% coated-EPS matrix, 0.5% PP 
fiber emerged as the practical optimum lifting toughness from 
14.71 to 18.24 mJ/mm³ (+24%) and flexural strength from 3.26 
to 3.59 MPa, while sacrificing only ~7% compressive strength. 
Pushing dosage to an extreme 2.0% confirmed this isn't a soft 
ceiling: compressive strength continued declining to 11.06 MPa 
and flexural strength fell below the unreinforced baseline, 
mathematically confirming a genuine degradation plateau rather 
than an artifact of a narrow test window.
Figure 7 : The line graphs showing the steep downward trendline of strength vs. 
fiber dosage
Conclusions
1.Optimized Output: 
The finalized ternary composite 10% RHA, 20% coated EPS, 
0.5% PP fiber achieves a lightweight density of 1993 kg/m³ (13% 
lighter than the plain control) while retaining 15.62 MPa 
compressive strength, 3.59 MPa flexural strength, and 18.24 
mJ/mm³ toughness, with porosity essentially unchanged from the 
dense baseline.
2.Scientific Validation: 
Macro-mechanical testing and micro-
analytical SEM–EDS diagnostics tell the 
same story from two independent directions 
porosity refinement and ITZ densification 
are the true strength-controlling 
mechanisms, not incidental correlations.
3.Real-World Viability: 
This ternary design methodology is 
generalizable and cost-effective, offering 
a practical route to lighter, more 
transportable precast ferrocement wall 
panels without the strength penalty that 
has historically limited EPS mortar adoption.
Mix optimization was carried out using Response Surface 
Methodology (RSM) with a Face-Centered Central Composite 
Design (CCF), allowing each mechanism to be isolated and 
statistically validated before combining them into the final 
ternary formulation.
Phase 1: Chemical Healing (Ball-Milled RHA) 
Rice Husk Ash was ball-milled to a reactive pozzolanic fineness, 
driving a secondary hydration reaction that consumes weak 
Calcium Hydroxide and deposits dense Calcium Silicate Hydrate 
(C-S-H) gel directly at the aggregate–paste boundary.
Figure 1 : Processed RHA powder
Phase 2: Physical Interlocking (Coated EPS) 
EPS beads were surface-coated using a Polyvinyl Acetate 
(PVAc) and M-sand matrix. This neutralized buoyancy 
(preventing segregation) and provided a roughened surface for 
mechanical interlocking with the cement paste.
Figure 2 : Coated EPS beads vs RAW EPS beads
Phase 3: Mechanical Toughening (PP Micro-Fibers) 
Polypropylene micro-fibers were dosed incrementally (0–2.0% 
by volume) into the optimized RHA–coated-EPS matrix to 
bridge micro-cracks and extend post-peak ductility, up to the 
threshold where geometric "fiber balling" begins to reverse the 
gains.
