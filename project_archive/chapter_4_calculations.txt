Conventional liquid pycnometry is unviable for raw EPS beads due to their extreme

3.2.2. Design of Experiments (DoE) & Factorial Mix Apportionment 
The multi-variable optimization matrix of this research was configured via Response 
Surface Methodology (RSM) driven by a Face - Centered Central Composite Design 
(CCF) framework. Traditional one-factor-at-a-time (OFAT) experimental approaches 
rely on an inefficient trial-and-error methodology that alters a single variable while 
holding others constant. This OFAT approach is fundamentally incapable of detecting 

========================================
<<< PDF PAGE 61 >>>
========================================
 
44 
 
non-linear interactions between conflicting variables such as the simultaneous 
workability loss caused by combining highly porous Rice Husk Ash (RHA) with high-
aspect-ratio Polypropylene (PP) micro-fibers. RSM circumvents this limitation by 
deploying a quadratic regression matrix that maps interactive contour gradients, 
accurately identifying the synergistic or antagonistic relationships between materials 
while drastically reducing the number of required experimental runs compared to full 
factorial designs. 
The optimization routine was executed across a sequential five-phase timeline. For 
each separate phase involving multiple variables, the CCF framework dictates exactly 
11 distinct mix configurations. This mathematical matrix comprises four factorial 
design points to map linear trends, four axial star points to detect curvature boundaries, 
and three identical center points to isolate experimental error variance and guarantee 
statistical repeatability. Critically, a Face - Centered Central Composite Design (CCF) 
was adopted, setting the axial star point distance to 𝛼= ±1. Extending the axial 
bounds to the traditional rotatable geometry (𝛼= ±1.414) was physically unviable; 
preliminary mixing indicated that exceeding the 𝛼= ±1boundary generated extreme 
factorial limits that yielded entirely unworkable matrices either inducing extreme 
stiffness that prevented compaction, or excessive fluidity that triggered immediate 
lightweight aggregate segregation. 
To maintain strict volumetric accounting across all replacement loops, the mix designs 
were proportioned using the Absolute Volume Method. When introducing ultra-low-
density inclusions like Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) beads, conventional mass-based 
batching fails because the massive density mismatch destroys the volumetric yield of 
the fresh mixture. The Absolute Volume Method ensures that the total combination of 
all constituent volumes equals exactly 1.0 m3of green mortar:

3.2.6. Macro-Physical and Destructive Performance Testing 
At exactly 28 days of hydration, the cured 40 × 40 × 160 mmmortar prisms were

The prisms were tested for three-point flexural strength (𝑓𝑟) using an automated

vertical load was applied through a central loading platen at a standardized load rate

load (𝐹𝑓) was recorded to compute the flexural capacity.

Following flexural splitting, the two resulting prism half-beams were immediately 
recovered to determine their compressive strength (𝑓𝑐) per EN 196-1. The half-prism 
was positioned between high-hardness steel compression platens utilizing standard 
40 × 40 mmsquare bearing plates, ensuring an exact cross-sectional loading area of 
1600 mm2. A continuous hydraulic load was applied at a standardized rate of 2400 ±
200 N/suntil the specimen underwent ultimate structural crushing. The maximum load 
at failure (𝐹𝑐) was logged to calculate the compressive capacity.

To examine the aggregate boundaries and validate mineral phase changes at the

3.2.7.1.Micro-Specimen Extraction and Hydration Arrest

low-viscosity epoxy resin blocks, and mechanically polished using progressive silicon 
carbide abrasive papers and diamond paste compounds.

blocks were mounted onto aluminum stubs using conductive carbon tape and placed 
inside a vacuum sputtering chamber. The assemblies were coated with a thin layer of 
high-purity gold to ensure stable electron beam interaction and prevent thermal 
distortion during high-resolution imaging.
