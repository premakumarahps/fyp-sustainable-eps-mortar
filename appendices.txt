5.6.1. Flexural Toughness and the Crack-Bridging Mechanism 
The flexural strength (𝑓𝑟) and toughness data clearly validate the necessity of 
discontinuous micro-reinforcement. In the unreinforced baseline (S5M1), advancing 
micro-cracks propagate rapidly through the brittle matrix, yielding a flexural capacity 
of just 3.26 MPa and a baseline toughness of 14.71 mJ/mm³. 
The introduction of PP micro-fibers establishes a randomly distributed, three-
dimensional crack-bridging web. When a micro-crack begins to propagate, it 
immediately intersects this dense fibrous matrix. The high tensile capacity of the PP 
filaments absorbs the strain energy, transferring the internal tensile stresses back into 
the surrounding dense paste through interfacial friction. 
This stress-transfer mechanism is highly effective. As illustrated in Figure 33 (Flexural 
Strength vs. PP Fiber and Toughness vs. PP Fiber), adding fibers creates an immediate 
upward trajectory. At a 0.5% dosage (S5M2), flexural strength increases to 3.59 MPa, 
and handling toughness sees a significant surge to 18.24 mJ/mm³. Flexural capacity 
peaks at 4.00 MPa at the 1.0% dosage (S5M3) before dropping back to 3.65 MPa at 
the extreme 1.5% threshold (S5M4) due to matrix disruption. Macroscopically, this 
fiber integration shifts the failure profile of the panel from sudden, catastrophic brittle 
rupture to controlled, ductile deformation, ensuring the composite can survive the 
dynamic stresses of factory demolding and transit. Beyond this window, however, the 
flexural gains reverse: at 2.0% (S5M5), flexural strength falls to 2.89 MPa below even 
the unreinforced baseline of 3.26 MPa while toughness continues climbing to its 
highest recorded value of 31.54 mJ/mm³. This confirms that past roughly 1.0% fiber

5.6.2. Compressive Capacity and the Fiber Balling Limit 
While flexural capacity scales positively with increased fiber dosage, the compressive 
strength (𝑓𝑐) data reveals a critical structural threshold that dictates the absolute 
optimal mix design. 
• 
The Optimum Threshold (0.5%):  
At the unreinforced baseline, the composite boasts a compressive strength of 
16.78 MPa. Replacing matrix volume with flexible polymer fibers inherently 
reduces this peak density. However, at the 0.5% volume fraction (S5M2), the 
composite safely retains a robust 15.62 MPa. At this low dosage, the fibers 
remain uniformly dispersed. They effectively sew together internal plastic 
shrinkage cracks during the early curing stages without heavily disrupting the 
dense packing of the cementitious paste, thereby preserving the required load-
bearing capacity for precast applications. 
• 
Structural Degradation (1.0% to 1.5%):  
Exceeding the 0.5% threshold triggers a severe mechanical penalty, clearly 
visible as a steep downward trendline in Figure 33. At 1.0% (S5M3), 
compressive strength drops to 14.14 MPa. At 1.5% (S5M4), the capacity 
plummets further to 11.85 MPa, and by 2.0% (S5M5) it declines only slightly 
further to 11.06 MPa indicating the matrix is approaching a degradation plateau 
rather than continuing to collapse at the earlier rate. This rapid degradation is 
driven by the geometric "fiber balling" effect. High concentrations of micro-
fibers drastically reduce fresh-state workability, tangling together to trap 
localized pockets of air during mixing. These entangled fiber masses create 
large, unfillable macroscopic voids within the internal structure. Under 
compressive loading, these voids act as critical stress concentrators, initiating 
premature matrix collapse.

6. CONCLUSION 
6.1.Summary of the Research 
This comprehensive investigation successfully engineered a high-performance, 
lightweight cementitious composite specifically optimized for the mass production of 
non-load-bearing precast partitioning panels. By systematically addressing the 
inherent structural liabilities of Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) inclusion, the research 
transitioned beyond traditional binary substitutions to establish a highly synergistic 
ternary matrix. Utilizing locally accessible Sri Lankan raw materials specifically Rice 
Husk Ash (RHA) and Manufactured Sand (M-sand) the study mitigated the structural 
dead-weight penalty of precast elements without sacrificing early-age handling 
resilience, fresh-state workability, or long-term mechanical stability. 
6.2.Fulfillment of Primary Objectives 
The multi-phase empirical testing matrix successfully satisfied the two core objectives 
established at the onset of this research, yielding the following definitive conclusions: 
• 
Objective 1: To quantify the independent and synergistic effects of Coated 
EPS, RHA, and PP fiber additions on lightweight mortar performance. 
o Independent Effects Validated: The isolated diagnostic phases 
successfully quantified the individual microstructural and mechanical 
contributions of each material. Phase 3 (Chemical Validation) proved via 
EDS/SEM analysis that RHA independently acts as a chemical welding 
agent, reducing the elemental Ca/Si ratio at the aggregate boundary by 
over 77% and precipitating dense C-S-H gels. Phase 4 (Physical 
Validation) demonstrated that the PVAc/M-sand coating independently 
increases aggregate specific gravity by 1000%, neutralizing the geometric 
"wall effect" and recovering up to 70% of lost compressive capacity. 
Finally, Phase 5 established that a 0.5% monofilament PP fiber addition 
independently increases flexural ductility by 10.1% by establishing a 3D 
crack-bridging web.

o Synergistic Equilibrium Quantified: The statistical Phase 2 optimization 
successfully mapped the non-linear synergy between these competing 
variables. An absolute ternary equilibrium was isolated at a 10% RHA 
mass replacement and a 20% coated-EPS volume fraction. This specific 
synergistic alignment successfully shed nearly 300 𝑘𝑔/𝑚3of structural 
dead-weight while maintaining a highly stable microstructural void

• 
Objective 2: To deliver a generalizable design methodology and validation

o Methodology and Dataset Delivered: By transitioning away from

accurately predict macroscopic compressive strength, flexural capacity, 
apparent porosity, and structural toughness based on precise physical input 
parameters. This dataset directly bridges the empirical validation vacuum 
within the regional precast sector, providing industrial engineers with a 
reliable, generalizable design tool to scale the production of sustainable, 
lightweight partitioning panels.

Beyond fulfilling the primary mechanical criteria, the progressive testing methodology 
revealed several critical engineering dynamics that push the boundaries of current
