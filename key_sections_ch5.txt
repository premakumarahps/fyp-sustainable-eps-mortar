discontinuous micro-reinforcement. In the unreinforced baseline (S5M1), advancing 
micro-cracks propagate rapidly through the brittle matrix, yielding a flexural capacity 
of just 3.26 MPa and a baseline toughness of 14.71 mJ/mm³. 
The introduction of PP micro-fibers establishes a randomly distributed, three-
dimensional crack-bridging web. When a micro-crack begins to propagate, it 
immediately intersects this dense fibrous matrix. The high tensile capacity of the PP 
filaments absorbs the strain energy, transferring the internal tensile stresses back into 
the surrounding dense paste through interfacial friction. 
This stress-transfer mechanism is highly effective. As illustrated in Figure 33 (Flexural

upward trajectory. At a 0.5% dosage (S5M2), flexural strength increases to 3.59 MPa,

peaks at 4.00 MPa at the 1.0% dosage (S5M3) before dropping back to 3.65 MPa at 
the extreme 1.5% threshold (S5M4) due to matrix disruption. Macroscopically, this

5.6.1. Flexural Toughness and the Crack-Bridging Mechanism 
The flexural strength (𝑓𝑟) and toughness data clearly validate the necessity of 
discontinuous micro-reinforcement. In the unreinforced baseline (S5M1), advancing 
micro-cracks propagate rapidly through the brittle matrix, yielding a flexural capacity 
of just 3.26 MPa and a baseline toughness of 14.71 mJ/mm³. 
The introduction of PP micro-fibers establishes a randomly distributed, three-
dimensional crack-bridging web. When a micro-crack begins to propagate, it 
immediately intersects this dense fibrous matrix. The high tensile capacity of the PP 
filaments absorbs the strain energy, transferring the internal tensile stresses back into 
the surrounding dense paste through interfacial friction. 
This stress-transfer mechanism is highly effective. As illustrated in Figure 33 (Flexural 
Strength vs. PP Fiber and Toughness vs. PP Fiber), adding fibers creates an immediate 
upward trajectory. At a 0.5% dosage (S5M2), flexural strength increases to 3.59 MPa, 
and handling toughness sees a significant surge to 18.24 mJ/mm³. Flexural capacity 
peaks at 4.00 MPa at the 1.0% dosage (S5M3) before dropping back to 3.65 MPa at 
the extreme 1.5% threshold (S5M4) due to matrix disruption. Macroscopically, this 
fiber integration shifts the failure profile of the panel from sudden, catastrophic brittle 
rupture to controlled, ductile deformation, ensuring the composite can survive the 
dynamic stresses of factory demolding and transit. Beyond this window, however, the 
flexural gains reverse: at 2.0% (S5M5), flexural strength falls to 2.89 MPa below even 
the unreinforced baseline of 3.26 MPa while toughness continues climbing to its 
highest recorded value of 31.54 mJ/mm³. This confirms that past roughly 1.0% fiber

6. CONCLUSION 
6.1.Summary of the Research 
This comprehensive investigation successfully engineered a high-performance, 
lightweight cementitious composite specifically optimized for the mass production of 
non-load-bearing precast partitioning panels. By systematically addressing the 
inherent structural liabilities of Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) inclusion, the research 
transitioned beyond traditional binary substitutions to establish a highly synergistic 
ternary matrix. Utilizing locally accessible Sri Lankan raw materials specifically Rice 
Husk Ash (RHA) and Manufactured Sand (M-sand) the study mitigated the structural 
dead-weight penalty of precast elements without sacrificing early-age handling 
resilience, fresh-state workability, or long-term mechanical stability. 
6.2.Fulfillment of Primary Objectives 
The multi-phase empirical testing matrix successfully satisfied the two core objectives 
established at the onset of this research, yielding the following definitive conclusions:

6. CONCLUSION 
6.1.Summary of the Research 
This comprehensive investigation successfully engineered a high-performance, 
lightweight cementitious composite specifically optimized for the mass production of 
non-load-bearing precast partitioning panels. By systematically addressing the 
inherent structural liabilities of Expanded Polystyrene (EPS) inclusion, the research 
transitioned beyond traditional binary substitutions to establish a highly synergistic 
ternary matrix. Utilizing locally accessible Sri Lankan raw materials specifically Rice 
Husk Ash (RHA) and Manufactured Sand (M-sand) the study mitigated the structural 
dead-weight penalty of precast elements without sacrificing early-age handling 
resilience, fresh-state workability, or long-term mechanical stability.
