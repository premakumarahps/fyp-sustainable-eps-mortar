5.3.5. Phase 2 Conclusion: Identifying the Lightweight Optimum 
The primary objective of Phase 2 was to design a ternary composite that safely resolves 
the "dead-weight penalty" without inducing macroscopic segregation, catastrophic 
flexural failure, or unmanageable brittleness. 
The empirical dataset and the subsequent physical surface modeling definitively 
isolate the 10% RHA and 20% EPS configuration (replicated precisely in center points 
S2M9, S2M10, S2M11) as the optimal material equilibrium for precast applications. 
By locking the matrix at this specific coordinate, the composite achieves a critical 
balance: 
• 
Handling Efficiency: It sheds nearly 300 kg/m³ of dead-weight compared to 
the baseline, achieving a hardened density of ~1993 kg/m³, which directly 
mitigates crane payload limits and internal bending moments during transit.

5.4.Phase 3 Results: Microstructural and Chemical Interfacial Transition Zone 
(ITZ) Diagnostics 
To validate the mechanical hypotheses established during the macroscopic evaluations 
in Phase 2, the morphology and elemental composition of the Interfacial Transition 
Zone (ITZ) were analyzed utilizing Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) coupled 
with Energy Dispersive X-Ray Spectroscopy (EDS). The diagnostic focus was strictly 
isolated to the boundary between the cementitious paste and the PVAc-coated EPS 
beads. To account for inherent matrix non-homogeneity, spatial sampling was 
conducted across three distinct measurement locations per mix. The comparative 
analysis evaluates the non-pozzolanic baseline mix (S3M1: 40% EPS, 0% RHA) 
against the optimally modified pozzolanic mix (S3M2: 40% EPS, 20% RHA). 
5.4.1. Chemical Healing and ITZ Densification 
EDS elemental analysis provides definitive quantitative evidence of the pozzolanic 
healing mechanism at the aggregate boundary. The localized stoichiometry of the ITZ 
was mapped by quantifying the Calcium (Ca) and Silicon (Si) atomic percentages 
along the polymer-paste interface.
